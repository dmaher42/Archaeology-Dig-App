## 1. Core Story Update

The story should align more clearly with Ancient Egyptian-inspired afterlife belief while keeping the fictional memory mythology.

Ancient Egyptian-inspired structure:

1. Life in Egypt.
2. Death.
3. Preservation of the body through mummification.
4. Journey through the Duat.
5. Gates, guardians, trials, spells, names, offerings, and protection.
6. Judgement and weighing of the heart.
7. If judged worthy, eternal life in the Field of Reeds.

Lost Site Expedition's fictional addition:

> Judgement grants passage. Memory sustains eternity.

This fixes the earlier logic issue: tomb objects are not only needed to help a person reach judgement. They also sustain the person's identity, name, relationships, land, story, offerings, and place in the afterlife after judgement.

## 2. Memory Anchors After Judgement

The dead may have already crossed into the Field of Reeds, but memory anchors still matter.

Memory anchors preserve:

- name
- identity
- relationships
- body/self connection
- offerings
- story
- family
- land
- duties
- place in eternity

When tomb objects are stolen, broken, renamed, misinterpreted, sold, displayed as treasure, or separated from their proper context, the dead are changed in the afterlife.

The damage is not simply loss of treasure. It is corruption of self.

Effects of damaged memory anchors:

- names fade
- identities blur
- memories change
- family and land connections weaken
- offerings no longer reach properly
- peace in the Field of Reeds becomes unstable
- the dead risk a slow second death through forgetting and corruption

Key rule:

> The living did not only steal from tombs. They reached back into eternity and damaged those who had already earned peace.

---

## 3. Corrupted Afterlife

The afterlife has crossed a breaking point.

The Field of Reeds was meant to be a perfected reflection of Egypt: a peaceful, divine mirror of life along the Nile.

In the game's current mythology:

> The Field of Reeds was meant to be a perfected reflection of Egypt. The Lost Site is what remains when that reflection is cracked.

The afterlife is now corrupted because too many memory anchors have been stolen, broken, changed, or misremembered.

The Lost Site should feel like a broken mirror-world: familiar Egyptian forms, but damaged, unstable, hostile, and wrong.

The monsters and fierce deities that were once part of the Duat journey were not meant to enter the peaceful afterlife. Because memory boundaries are failing, dangers from the Duat have leaked into places of peace.

This explains combat:

- guardians have become confused or hostile
- Duat creatures have breached sacred boundaries
- seals are failing
- memory rooms are unstable
- the Queen's memory has been corrupted
- Anubis is furious because this should not be possible

Avoid calling this “hell” directly. It is better described as a corrupted Field of Reeds / damaged Duat-touched threshold / cracked mirror of Egypt.

---

## 4. What the Lost Site Is

The Lost Site is not the full Duat.

It is not the full Field of Reeds.

It is a sacred threshold between the living world, the Duat, memory, judgement, and the Field of Reeds.

Preferred language:

- The Lost Site — player-facing name
- Duat-touched threshold — lore term
- a threshold between breath and judgement — poetic explanation
- cracked mirror of Egypt — visual/worldbuilding idea
- House of Eternity — tomb/chamber language

Story rule:

> The Lost Site is a failing sacred threshold built to protect memory anchors and maintain the boundary between the living world, the Duat, and the Field of Reeds.

## 5. Why Asha Can Cross Alive

Only the dead should cross into the Duat/afterlife path.

Asha crossing alive should feel impossible and alarming.

Anubis' interpretation:

- a mortal stands beyond his seal
- this should not happen
- there is one road beyond life: death
- therefore humans must have found another way to break the boundary
- because humans have always taken, he assumes this breach is motivated by greed

True reason:

The Queen's final uncorrupted action was to call outward to the living world because the dead could no longer repair what the living had broken.

The scarab appearing in the real world was not treasure and not a normal object.

It was:

- a broken warning
- a threshold seal
- a call for help
- a doorway opening incorrectly because the system is failing

Asha was not chosen in a simple destiny sense. The scarab reaches her because:

1. she is connected to the Queen's final scribe / keeper of names line
2. she notices the evidence others dismissed
3. she values artefacts for history, story, preservation, and respect rather than ownership or greed

Important line:

> The seal knew her bloodline. It judged her by her hands.

## 6. Asha's Values and Family Connection

Keep Asha connected to the family tree, but do not make bloodline the whole reason she matters.

Preferred current direction:

Asha descends from the Queen's final scribe / keeper of names, the person who recorded or preserved the Queen's true story before it was damaged, hidden, or erased.

But Asha can cross and remain whole because of more than blood.

She has always valued artefacts and tomb evidence for:

- history
- stories
- identity
- people
- preservation
- what they reveal about life, death, belief, and memory

She believes tombs should not be disturbed and objects should not be removed for ownership or profit.

She does not yet understand the hidden truth that artefacts are literally memory anchors, but her values already align with that truth.

Story rule:

> Blood opened the door. Values kept her whole. Actions will decide whether she is trusted.

---

## 7. Anubis' Early View of Asha

Anubis should not trust Asha in the first 2-3 rooms.

He has already judged humanity as corrupt, greedy, forgetful, and unworthy.

He is not neutral when Asha arrives.

He believes:

- humans forgot the journey
- humans forgot the scales
- humans forgot the dead
- humans broke tombs
- humans broke names
- humans broke memory
- humans called taking discovery
- humans may now have broken the boundary itself

For the first several rooms, Anubis should interpret Asha's repairs as self-serving.

He believes she is restoring seals, rituals, and murals only because doing so opens gates, portals, or routes deeper into the Lost Site.

He does not trust words.

He judges actions.

His judgement should only begin to shift after Asha protects memory without immediate personal gain.

Standing rule:

> For the first several Egypt rooms, Anubis interprets Asha's repairs as self-serving. He believes she is restoring seals, rituals, and murals only because doing so opens gates, portals, or routes deeper into the Lost Site. His judgement begins to shift only when Asha protects memory without immediate reward or personal gain.

## 8. Prologue / Arrival Direction Still Needs Implementation

The prologue still needs adjusting.

There are three separate opening pieces and they must not be confused:

1. Cairo archive prologue: Asha investigates the scarab through evidence and goes to the real-world pyramid.
2. Lost Site opening cinematic: Asha arrives in the mirror-world / Duat-touched threshold and first confronts Anubis.
3. First seal monologue: the later `SCARAB_SEAL_TRIGGER` warning where Anubis says “Leave.”

The new prologue discussion mainly affects item 2: the Lost Site opening cinematic.

### Current intended Lost Site opening cinematic direction

Asha should help the player understand the setting.

Anubis should not explain the story. He only reacts with shock, hostility, sacred authority, and judgement.

Asha may infer, from her knowledge of ancient Egypt, that this place resembles the Duat or a threshold connected to it. She remains confused about where she is, why she is there, and why she is alive there.

Anubis is shocked because a living mortal should not be beyond the seal.
