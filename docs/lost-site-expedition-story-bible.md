# Lost Site Expedition Story Bible

This document is the current source of truth for the premium story direction of **Lost Site Expedition**.

It should guide future narrative, room, gate, relic, dialogue, and gameplay decisions. Do not replace this foundation with a separate story system unless the story direction is deliberately revised.

---

## 1. Core Premise

**Lost Site Expedition** is a present-day historical mystery adventure where Asha, a young heritage researcher, discovers that ancient Egyptian relics are not just objects from the past. They are anchors that help maintain balance between the living world, ancient memory, and the afterlife.

When sacred relics are stolen, damaged, sold, or removed from their proper place, the hidden world connected to them begins to break.

The central theme is:

> The past is not treasure to own. It is memory to protect.

---

## 2. Date and World

The real-world story begins in the **present day**.

Asha is not travelling through normal Egypt for the full game. After touching the scarab relic, she is pulled into the **Lost Site**: a hidden, sacred version of Egypt caught between the real world, ancient memory, and the afterlife.

This world can look ancient, supernatural, ruined, impossible, and mythic because it is not ordinary historical Egypt. It is a Duat-touched memory-world shaped by relics, seals, rituals, and broken history.

This explains why the game world can contain:

- ancient Egyptian structures
- impossible tomb spaces
- Anubis and sacred guardians
- relic-powered barriers
- broken seals
- supernatural enemies
- modern archaeological themes
- ancient memory and myth working together

The world is not merely a ruin. It is a failing sacred system.

---

## 3. Who Asha Is

Asha is a young **heritage researcher / archaeologist-in-training** connected to a museum, university, or excavation project.

She is not a treasure hunter.
She is not a soldier.
She is not looking for personal glory.

At the start of the story, Asha believes the past can be understood through evidence, research, and careful investigation. She respects history, but she does not yet understand the full sacred weight of what she is entering.

Her arc moves from:

> I study the past.

To:

> I protect what remains of it.

---

## 4. Why Asha Enters the Lost Site

Asha notices that certain Egyptian relics are changing.

Possible signs include:

- an inscription shifts meaning after being translated
- a scarab seal pulses with light or heat
- a museum object displays damage that was not there before
- an excavation photograph shows a doorway that no longer exists
- a stolen relic is returned damaged, and the same damage appears in an ancient image or mural
- records from different archives begin contradicting each other

Asha realises the problem is not only that relics are being stolen.

The deeper problem is:

> The past is being damaged and rewritten.

When she touches the scarab relic, she is pulled into the Lost Site. Anubis notices immediately.

---

## 5. Why Asha Looks Partly Modern and Partly Ancient

Asha begins as a present-day researcher, but when she enters the Lost Site, the world marks her.

Her visual design should sit between worlds:

- modern expedition gear
- practical researcher clothing
- field belt and pouches
- Egyptian-inspired teal, gold, or scarab details
- relic markings or transformed accessories
- wrapped guards or protective elements
- an ancient weapon or relic blade if required by gameplay

She is not simply wearing an ancient Egyptian costume.

The Lost Site has reshaped her appearance into something that belongs between the modern world and the sacred memory-world.

Asha should visually communicate:

- researcher
- survivor
- intruder
- chosen heir or marked descendant
- future protector

---

## 6. Who Anubis Is

Anubis is the guardian of the Lost Site.

He protects sacred relics, treasures, tombs, names, memories, and the balance between the living and the dead.

Historically inspired Anubis should not be written as a god who simply hates humanity. He is a judge, guardian, and protector. In this story, he is deeply disappointed in humanity because of what he has witnessed for centuries.

He has watched humans:

- steal from tombs
- sell sacred objects
- break seals
- remove relics from their proper places
- claim to seek truth while chasing glory
- call taking things discovery
- misunderstand the dead
- disturb what should have been protected

Anubis has judged humanity by its actions.

His starting position is:

> Humans always take.

When Asha arrives, he assumes she is another trespasser.

He does not trust her words. He watches what she does.

---

## 7. Anubis' Opening Voice

At the beginning, Anubis should sound powerful, intimidating, ancient, short, sharp, and unwilling to debate.

He is not a guide yet.
He is not a mentor yet.
He does not want Asha there.

The current intended opening tone is:

> You stand where you should not.
>  
> Even pharaohs tread carefully within these halls.
>  
> They knew what rested here.
>  
> You do not.
>  
> For centuries I have watched your kind.
>  
> The faces change.
>  
> The greed does not.
>  
> Leave.

This is not hatred. It is judgement.

Anubis should sound like an ancient guardian who has already reached a verdict on humanity and expects Asha to prove him right.

---

## 8. What Is Happening to the Lost Site

The Lost Site is failing.

Its memory seals are breaking because sacred relics have been stolen, damaged, or corrupted in the real world.

The seals do not only lock doors. They protect memory, truth, names, rituals, and balance.

When relics are removed from their proper place, the damage spreads into the hidden world.

This creates the central mystery:

- Why are the seals failing?
- What did the Queen really do?
- Why has the evidence been damaged or altered?
- What has Anubis misunderstood?
- Why can Asha enter when others should be kept out?

Anubis senses something is wrong, but he does not want to believe humans can be part of the solution.

---

## 9. Relics, Looting, and Memory Seals

Sacred relics maintain balance.

They are not ordinary collectibles.
They are not simply treasure.
They are anchors for memory and protection.

When relics are stolen or broken:

- seals weaken
- rooms become unstable
- guardians awaken
- history becomes harder to read
- evidence contradicts itself
- the Lost Site loses pieces of its truth

This links the story to archaeology and historical thinking:

- evidence can be incomplete
- evidence can be damaged
- evidence can be misinterpreted
- different sources can conflict
- the past must be carefully pieced together

In the game, this should feel like mystery and consequence, not a lesson.

---

## 10. Asha's Hidden Connection

Asha may be connected to a forgotten line of seal-keepers.

This should not simply mean she is royal or special because of bloodline. Her connection should be tied to truth, protection, and evidence.

The strongest current direction is:

> Asha is descended from the Queen's final scribe, a priestess, a tomb protector, or a keeper of names who recorded the truth of the Queen's final act.

This gives Asha a meaningful connection to:

- the Scribe Chamber
- written evidence
- lost records
- damaged truth
- the Queen's real story
- the restoration of memory seals

Asha's importance should come from her choices and actions, not only ancestry.

---

## 11. The Queen

The Queen is always the final major chamber and the mandatory mini-boss encounter.

She should not be treated as a simple evil boss.

At first, the evidence may suggest she caused the disaster. The site may frame her as dangerous, cursed, greedy, or responsible for sealing something terrible away.

But the truth should become more complicated.

Possible final direction:

- the Queen was blamed for the collapse
- the Queen sacrificed her name, legacy, or memory to protect the site
- the Queen sealed away a greater danger
- the Queen's story was damaged, erased, or rewritten
- the final fight is against a corrupted memory of the Queen, not necessarily her true self

The Queen is the centre of the contested interpretation.

The game should ask:

> Was the Queen a villain, a protector, a warning, or a victim of broken evidence?

---

## 12. Room Order

The current intended premium room order is:

1. **Mummification Chamber**
2. **Mural Room**
3. **Scribe Chamber**
4. **Queen Chamber**

The Queen Chamber is always last, mandatory, and connected to the mini-boss/final major encounter.

The first three rooms should be optional but meaningful.

Players should be able to enter optional rooms before reaching nearby checkpoints or gates.

---

## 13. Room Story Purposes

### Mummification Chamber

Core meaning:

- preservation
- respect for the dead
- burial practice
- human remains
- sacred care

Narrative purpose:

Asha learns that the site is not protecting treasure. It is protecting people, rituals, and memory.

This room should challenge players to slow down and treat the dead with care.

### Mural Room

Core meaning:

- visual evidence
- erased memory
- damaged interpretation
- symbolism
- conflicting clues

Narrative purpose:

Asha discovers that the walls are telling a story, but parts of the story have been deliberately damaged or hidden.

This room should make players question what the evidence is showing and what has been removed.

### Scribe Chamber

Core meaning:

- written evidence
- records
- translation
- contradiction
- historical interpretation

Narrative purpose:

Asha finds written records that complicate the story told by the murals.

This room should show that written evidence can preserve truth, but can also be incomplete, biased, hidden, or misunderstood.

### Queen Chamber

Core meaning:

- final contested truth
- judgement
- corrupted memory
- the Queen's last act
- mandatory confrontation

Narrative purpose:

Asha confronts the version of the Queen that the broken site has preserved. The final encounter should reveal that the simple story was incomplete.

---

## 14. Gates and Checkpoints

Route gates should exist to the right of major structures as pacing tools and Anubis-controlled thresholds.

They should slow the player down, create checkpoints, and encourage the player to look back or consider entering nearby rooms.

However, the optional rooms should not be required for main progression yet.

Current gate rule:

> Gates should unlock through required main-path objectives, not optional room completion.

Narrative meaning:

> Anubis is not granting trust. He is allowing Asha to continue because she survived or completed a required trial.

Gates should feel like judgement thresholds:

- You survived this part of the site.
- You may continue, if you insist.
- This does not mean you are trusted.

Optional rooms should remain future judgement evidence, not current forced progression.

---

## 15. Heart Scarabs and Future Moral Choice

A future system may introduce a sacred collectible/currency tentatively called **Heart Scarabs**.

Heart Scarabs are valuable sacred fragments connected to the site's broken memory seals.

They may eventually be used in two ways:

1. **Spend them on Asha** for upgrades, strength, tools, or survival.
2. **Return them to the site** to restore broken seals and repair memory.

This creates the central moral pressure:

> Does Asha use sacred power for herself, or return it to restore balance?

This should not be implemented fully yet.

Do not add a morality system, trust meter, alternate ending system, or upgrade economy until the room, gate, and seal structure is stable.

---

## 16. Anubis' Judgement Arc

Anubis does not trust Asha's words.

He judges her actions.

The long-term arc should move roughly like this:

### Beginning

Anubis sees Asha as another trespasser.

### Early Journey

Asha survives trials, but Anubis still assumes survival is not the same as worthiness.

### Optional Rooms

If Asha chooses to enter optional rooms, restore seals, or return sacred fragments, Anubis begins to see evidence that she may be different.

### Late Journey

Anubis is forced to reconsider his judgement.

### Finale

Asha's actions help determine whether Anubis sees her as another human who took from the dead, or someone who chose to protect memory.

This should eventually be tracked as **judgement evidence**, not a simple morality bar.

---

## 17. Current Implementation Rules

These rules should guide near-term development:

- Do not create duplicate dialogue systems.
- Do not create duplicate gate systems.
- Do not create a trust meter yet.
- Do not create branching endings yet.
- Do not force optional room completion for main gate progression yet.
- Do not turn Anubis into a friendly guide too early.
- Do not make the Queen a simple evil boss without mystery.
- Do not treat relics as ordinary loot.
- Do not present educational ideas as direct classroom exposition.

The game should teach through mystery, choice, evidence, environment, and consequence.

---

## 18. Premium Story Standard

Every new story element should do at least one of the following:

1. Reveal something about the Lost Site.
2. Show Asha learning, questioning, or changing.
3. Build Anubis' suspicion, judgement, or reluctant reconsideration.
4. Make the player curious to keep exploring.
5. Connect to archaeology, evidence, preservation, memory, or interpretation.
6. Support the theme that the past is memory to protect, not treasure to own.

Avoid prototype-style writing such as:

- Find the key.
- Open the door.
- Collect the relic.
- Complete the puzzle.
- This mural shows ancient Egyptians.

Prefer story-driven writing such as:

- The seal trembles, but does not release her.
- This was not dropped. It was placed.
- The painted hands point toward a name that has been scratched away.
- The record survives, but the truth has been wounded.
- The dead were not hidden here. They were entrusted here.

---

## 19. Short Reference Summary

**Setting:** Present day, then a hidden Duat-touched version of Egypt.

**Asha:** Young heritage researcher / archaeologist-in-training.

**Inciting event:** A scarab relic reacts and pulls Asha into the Lost Site.

**Anubis:** Ancient guardian, disappointed judge of humanity, protector of sacred memory.

**Problem:** Looting and damaged relics have weakened memory seals and destabilised the Lost Site.

**Asha's possible connection:** Descendant of a forgotten seal-keeper, scribe, priestess, tomb protector, or keeper of names.

**Rooms:** Mummification, Mural, Scribe, Queen.

**Queen:** Mandatory final chamber and centre of the contested truth.

**Future moral pressure:** Spend sacred power on yourself or return it to restore balance.

**Theme:** The past is not treasure to own. It is memory to protect.
